# AI Use Case Hub

## Purpose

The **AI Use Case Hub** is a central platform for Cyber colleagues to identify and share day-to-day pain points, explore opportunities for improvement, and turn high-value problems into practical AI use cases.

The principle is simple:

**People do not need to come with an AI solution. They only need to bring a problem worth solving.**

The Hub will help transform those problems into structured use cases, assess their potential value, identify common challenges across Cyber, and provide an end-to-end path from **Idea → Review → PoC → Pilot → Scale**.

---

## The Problem We Want to Solve

People across Cyber experience repetitive, manual and inefficient processes every day. However:

- People may not know whether AI can help.
- Valuable pain points are often discussed locally but never captured.
- Different teams may be trying to solve the same problem independently.
- Traditional idea submission forms discourage participation.
- There is no single place to track an AI opportunity from initial idea through PoC and pilot.
- Leadership has limited visibility of common pain points across Cyber and where AI investment could deliver the greatest value.

The AI Use Case Hub aims to close this gap.

---

## 1. Simple, AI-Assisted Idea Submission

Instead of asking colleagues to complete a long form, the Hub will provide a conversational experience.

Users simply describe their work and pain point in their own words.

For example:

> “Our team manually reviews traffic every time an application needs a new network policy. It requires several people, takes several days and involves repetitive analysis.”

AI will understand the description, ask relevant follow-up questions where information is missing, and automatically structure it into a use case including:

- Function / Team
- Application or service
- Problem statement
- Current process
- Key bottleneck
- People impacted
- Frequency
- Current effort
- Expected benefit
- One Metric That Matters (OMTM)
- Contact point

The user reviews and confirms the AI-generated summary before submission.

---

## 2. Discover Existing Ideas

The Homepage will also allow colleagues to explore ideas already submitted across Cyber.

Ideas can be searched and filtered by:

**Function**

- SOC
- Cyber Resilience
- IAM
- Network Security
- Cloud Security
- Vulnerability Management
- Other Cyber functions

**Use Case Category**

- Automation
- Investigation & Triage
- Knowledge & Search
- Reporting
- Data Analysis
- Decision Support
- Self-Service
- Monitoring & Detection
- Workflow Optimisation

This allows people to discover whether another team is already experiencing or solving a similar problem.

---

## 3. Find Common Problems Across Cyber

The Hub should not simply collect hundreds of independent ideas.

AI will compare new submissions with existing ideas and identify potentially similar use cases.

Where a similar idea already exists, the user can:

- **Contribute to the existing use case**
- **Continue with a separate idea**
- **Ask AI to explain the similarities and differences**

This allows multiple individual ideas to contribute towards a common Cyber use case without losing the original contributors or context.

For example:

**Idea A — SOC investigation requires manual data collection**

**Idea B — Resilience investigation requires correlation across multiple monitoring platforms**

These may ultimately contribute towards a broader:

**AI-Assisted Investigation Use Case**

This gives leadership visibility not only of how many ideas have been submitted, but also which problems are repeatedly experienced across different Cyber functions.

---

## 4. From Individual Idea to Cyber Use Case

An **Idea** and a **Use Case** will be treated separately.

An Idea represents an individual pain point or contribution.

A Use Case represents a consolidated opportunity that Cyber may decide to pursue.

Therefore:

**Idea 001 + Idea 017 + Idea 023 → Use Case 008**

All original contributors and requirements remain recorded.

The Use Case can have clearly defined roles including:

- Contributors
- Business Owner
- Use Case Lead
- Sponsor / Approver
- Technical Lead
- Developer

---

## 5. Value-Based Review

Every use case should identify a measurable outcome rather than simply describing an interesting AI capability.

Each use case will therefore define a primary:

### One Metric That Matters (OMTM)

**Alert Suppression**  
Current turnaround: 5.4 days  
Target: <1 day

**Network Policy Creation**  
Current turnaround: ~5 days  
Target: <2 hours

Leadership can assess opportunities based on evidence such as:

- Business impact
- Organizational reach
- Current effort
- Frequency
- Expected benefit
- OMTM
- Technical feasibility
- Risk and dependencies

The intention is to avoid prioritising ideas simply because the technology appears innovative.

---

## 6. Leadership Review

Leadership will have a dedicated view of the AI opportunity pipeline.

Typical stages:

**Submitted → Under Review → Approved for PoC → PoC → Pilot → Scale / Production**

Review decisions can include:

- Approved for PoC
- More Information Required
- Hold
- Not Proceed

Reviewer, date, comments and decision rationale will be retained to provide governance and traceability.

---

## 7. From Idea to Project Workspace

Once an idea is approved for PoC, the same use case evolves into a project workspace.

The workspace can contain:

- Business Case
- Business Requirements
- Architecture
- Technical Design
- PoC Definition
- Success Criteria
- PoC Results
- Pilot Information
- Documents
- Decisions
- Ownership
- Activity History

This creates a continuous record from the original pain point through delivery.

---

## 8. AI-Assisted Requirements

AI can continue supporting the use case after approval.

Using the original pain point and business case as context, AI can work with the business contact to capture more detailed requirements and generate a structured draft covering:

- Current State
- Target State
- Personas
- Functional Requirements
- Non-Functional Requirements
- Integrations
- Data Requirements
- Security / RBAC
- Acceptance Criteria
- Out of Scope

These requirements remain subject to business and technical review before development.

The approved requirements can subsequently provide structured context for developers and AI-assisted development.

---

## 9. PoC and Pilot

A PoC should demonstrate a defined hypothesis rather than simply produce a working demo.

Each PoC will therefore record:

- PoC hypothesis
- Success criteria
- Technical approach
- Owner
- Target date
- Evidence
- Result

The next stage, Pilot, determines whether the solution delivers measurable value in a real working environment.

Where possible, the original OMTM will be measured again during the pilot to compare:

**Before → After**

This creates evidence for deciding whether the solution should be scaled.

---

## 10. Information Sharing and Access

The Hub should encourage knowledge sharing without unnecessarily exposing sensitive Cyber information.

A layered visibility model is proposed.

### Cyber-wide

Users can see:

- Use case title
- Problem statement
- Function
- Category
- Expected benefit
- OMTM
- Status
- Contributors
- Business owner

This supports discovery, collaboration and duplicate prevention.

### Project Workspace

Detailed information such as:

- Business requirements
- Technical requirements
- Architecture
- Integration details
- PoC documentation
- Technical design

will be restricted to appropriate project members, reviewers and stakeholders.

Highly sensitive material can have additional document-level access control.

---

## Strategic Outcome

Over time, the AI Use Case Hub can become more than an idea submission platform.

It can provide a Cyber-wide view of:

- Where manual effort is concentrated
- Which problems affect multiple functions
- Which AI opportunities have demonstrated measurable value
- Which PoCs successfully progressed to pilots
- What solutions already exist across Cyber
- Where investment could potentially have broader organisational impact

The goal is therefore not simply to answer:

**“How many AI ideas do we have?”**

but:

**“What are the most important problems across Cyber, and where can AI meaningfully improve how we work?”**
